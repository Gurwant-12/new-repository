About the Universe

The universe contains all of space and time, energy and matter. Within it, stars form in galaxies; planets form from disks of dust and gas around young stars. Our Solar System hosts a diverse family of worlds: rocky terrestrial planets, immense gas giants, frigid ice giants, and small dwarf planets beyond Neptune.

  • Terrestrial worlds: Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—small, rocky, with solid surfaces.
  • Gas giants: Jupiter and Saturn—massive, dominated by hydrogen and helium, with rings and many moons.
  • Ice giants: Uranus and Neptune—rich in volatiles like water, ammonia, and methane.
  • Dwarf planets: Bodies like Pluto—round but not dominant in their orbits.
